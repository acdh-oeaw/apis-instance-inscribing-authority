Inscribing Authority Database

Islamic Rule in Central Asia (10th–13th Centuries) as Reflected in Monumental Epigraphy

Inscribing Authority proposes the first comprehensive study of architectural inscriptions dating from the 10th – 13th centuries, recorded in an area encompassing present-day Afghanistan and the Central Asian republics (Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan).

These epigraphic sources, composed in Arabic or Persian, appear on secular and religious monuments, are produced in diverse materials, techniques and styles, and present heterogeneous content that resonates with the political discourses, religious currents, and literary and artistic trends of their time. Their study promises to shed light on the textual and visual communication strategies that shaped Islamic rule in the region, enabling temporal and religious authorities to appropriate space and gain recognition from local communities.

The database

The database gathers texts and descriptions of the inscriptions, photographs, graphic reconstructions, and any available contextual information. It allows us to map the inscriptions, define their architectural space and establish connections between monuments and historical figures (patrons, artisans, authors, etc.).More generally, it aims at reappraising the dual nature of inscriptions as texts and artifacts, materialised expressions of their socio-historical context. It seeks to make available a collection of little-known primary sources, facilitating interdisciplinary comparisons with manuscripts, coins, and material culture.

The dataset includes information on inscriptions, monuments, objects (intended as architectural elements or other inscribed artifacts), persons, works, and places, as well as references to primary and secondary sources that account for the relationships between these entities. At the present stage, the corpus is limited to inscriptions from the focus area that are confidently dated to the 10th – 13th century, both on the basis of their content and on archaeological grounds. The scope may be extended, chronologically or geographically, through further research.

Project and resources

The project Inscribing Authority is hosted at the Institute of Iranian Studies at the Austrian Academy of Sciences .

The database was developed with the technical expertise of the ACDH , using the Austrian Prosopographical/Biographical Information System (APIS) platform.

It is linked to the Zotero group library “Inscribing Authority” , which provides metadata for cited sources and illustration credits.

External references to the largest repository of Islamic inscriptions, the Thésaurus d’Épigraphie Islamique , are also provided.

Upon project completion, the dataset will be made available under open licenses.
